01 · The idea

Two scales that share two notes

Each pentatonic scale has five notes. The minor pentatonic is the root, b3, 4, 5 and b7. The major pentatonic is the root, 2, 3, 5 and 6. They share only the root and the 5th, and that gives you two safe places to land whichever scale you are in.

1

Minor pentatonic

The grit. The b3 leans against a major chord and gives the blues its bite. Players reach for it first.

2

Major pentatonic

The sweet side. It holds the 3rd and the 6th, so lines sound open and singing, close to country and gospel.

3

Shared notes

The root and the 5th live in both scales. They make clean landing spots while you change from one to the other.

4

Three one-fret swaps

The b3 moves up a fret to the 3rd. The 4th moves down a fret to the 3rd. The b7 moves down a fret to the 6th. A fret away is the strongest pull from lesson 2.

One example in A. A minor pentatonic is A, C, D, E, G. A major pentatonic is A, B, C#, E, F#. Over an A7 chord the 3rd is C#. The C in the minor box is a blue note that leans up a fret to it. Slide from C to C# and you have moved from one scale to the other.

02 · The two scales

Both scales on the neck, one box at a time

Pick a key and a box. The minor box shows in one set of dots and the major box next to it shows in another. The major box sits within a fret of its minor neighbor, so your hand barely moves.

03 · The box next door

Why the major box sits three frets lower

A major pentatonic has the same five notes as the minor pentatonic whose root sits three frets below it. A major pentatonic is the relative of that minor: A major pentatonic is F# minor pentatonic, with the same notes and a different home. The major box uses shapes you already know, shifted down three frets, and each one lands beside a minor box.

In A, minor box 1 sits at frets 5 to 8 and its major neighbor sits at frets 4 to 7. The two boxes share frets 5 to 7, so your hand stays put while your ear hears a new home. That overlap is the doorway between the two sounds.

05 · The 12-bar blues map

Where the major pentatonic gives you the chord's 3rd

Over a dominant chord the 3rd is the note that says "major." This table checks each chord of a 12-bar blues in your key and tells you which scale supplies that 3rd.

A simple plan follows. Start each chorus in the minor box. When the chord gives you a 3rd that the major pentatonic holds, move to the major box for a phrase. When you reach a chord whose 3rd is in neither scale, stay in the minor box and use the note a fret below the 3rd as the blue note that leans into it. Drop back to the minor box before the harmony turns, so the return feels earned.

Clapton and the switch

The B.B. King and Buddy Guy line

Guitar World's lesson on Clapton's sixties playing says he often alternated between minor and major pentatonic, following B.B. King and Buddy Guy. A Guitar World deep dive describes the minor-to-major 3rd bend or hammer as a standard part of his style.

"Crossroads" with Cream

The same lesson analyzes the "Crossroads" solo as a 12-bar blues in A built on A minor pentatonic, played high on the neck. Listen to how much of it stays in the minor box and where a note leans up toward the major.

"Steppin' Out"

That lesson's example solo over a fast G shuffle hammers from the minor 3rd to the major 3rd in bars 10 and 12. Listen for the moment the gritty note becomes the bright one.

I to IV and back

A Guitar World lesson shows players such as Clapton moving from the key's minor pentatonic over the I chord to its major pentatonic over the IV chord, because the major pentatonic holds the IV chord's 3rd.

07 · The pattern recognition

Four rules that carry to any blues

Swap by one fret

The b3 rises to the 3rd, the 4th falls to the 3rd and the b7 falls to the 6th. Each swap is one fret, so you can change scales in the middle of a phrase.

Minor for grit, major for sweet

Start in the minor box and bring in the major box when a phrase wants to sing. The contrast makes both sounds stronger.

Land on shared notes

The root and the 5th belong to both scales. End a phrase there and the listener never hears the seam.

Check the chord's 3rd

If the major pentatonic holds the chord's 3rd, it fits well over that chord. If neither scale holds it, play the minor box and treat the note a fret below as a blue note.

08 · Put it to work

Practice path

  1. Choose a key and find box 1 in both views. Play the minor box up and down, then the major box beside it.
  2. Play Move 1 and Move 4 back to back. Hear how the same fingers change the mood.
  3. Play Move 2 and Move 3 until the b3 to 3rd bend and slide feel natural.
  4. Loop a 12-bar blues backing track. Play the minor box for four bars, then the major box for one phrase at each chord whose 3rd is in the major pentatonic.
  5. Move to box 2 and repeat. Then change the key and run the five moves again.

09 · Quick answers

Questions about major and minor pentatonic on guitar

What is the difference between major and minor pentatonic?

The minor pentatonic is the root, b3, 4, 5 and b7. The major pentatonic is the root, 2, 3, 5 and 6. The minor sounds darker and bluesier, and the major sounds brighter and sweeter.

How do I switch from minor to major pentatonic?

Bend or slide the b3 up one fret to the 3rd, then continue in the major box that sits a fret or two below your minor box.

Which scale should I use over a blues?

Both work. Start with the minor pentatonic for the main sound and add the major pentatonic when you want a sweeter phrase, especially over chords whose 3rd it contains.

Is the major pentatonic the same as the relative minor pentatonic?

They share the same five notes. A major pentatonic uses the same shapes as the minor pentatonic three frets lower, with a different note as home.

Did Eric Clapton really play this way?

Published lessons describe him alternating between minor and major pentatonic, with the minor-to-major 3rd bend or hammer as a regular device. He followed B.B. King and Buddy Guy.

When should I switch scales?

Switch at a phrase boundary or when the chord changes to one whose 3rd the major pentatonic holds. Return to the minor box before the harmony turns again.
