01 · The idea

Tension pulls. Release lands.

Release notes are the root, the 3rd and the 5th. They sit still. A half step off a chord tone pulls back to it. A bigger clash pulls harder.

Hold the tense note a beat past comfort, then land on the root, 3rd or 5th. The landing is the release.

1

Release

The root, 3rd and 5th. Land here.

2

Color

The 7th, the 9th and the 6th. They sit next to the chord and still sound settled.

3

Tension

A scale note a half step above a chord tone, such as the 4th over a major or dominant chord. It clashes with the note below it and wants to drop onto it.

4

Outside

Notes outside the chord's scale. They pull the hardest. On a dominant chord, bend the flat 3rd up into the major 3rd.

Over C7 the notes are C, E, G and Bb. F sits a fret above E and rubs the 3rd. Play F, hold it, drop to E. That drop is the release. The same F over F is the root. The chord under the note decides the job.

06 · The long arc

Five dials

Notes are one dial. Rhythm, register, volume and repetition climb too. The release is the bar where they drop and you land.

1

Distance from the chord

Chord tones, then color, then tension and outside notes. Each step sits farther from the chord.

2

Rhythm

Long notes sit still. Quarters, then eighths, then sixteenths push even when the tempo holds.

3

Register

Higher notes push harder. Climb a few frets at a time. Save the top for the peak.

4

Volume and attack

Pick harder, bend wider, let it ring. Play softer when you land.

5

Repetition

Repeat one short phrase and squeeze it. The break in the pattern is the release.

07 · What to listen for

Trey and Rick

Listening prompts for a long jam. No transcription.

Trey Anastasio

Put on a long Phish jam. He repeats a short idea while the band lifts, then lands on a clear note when the section turns. Name that note. Name what he played two bars earlier. The flat 3rd bent up to the 3rd is the blues pull.

Rick Mitarotonda

In a Guitar Player interview he cites Sonny Rollins: keep pushing, then get out of the way. The push is the tense notes. Getting out of the way is the landing. His line settles. The band keeps moving.

09 · The pattern recognition

Four rules. Any song.

A fret away pulls hardest

A note one fret from a chord tone wants to move to it. Spot the nearest chord tone and you know where the line is heading.

The 4th leans down

Over a major or dominant chord, the 4th sits a fret above the 3rd. F over C7 drops to E. Hold the 4th for a beat, then let it fall.

The blue note leans up

The flat 3rd over a major or dominant chord, Eb over C7, bends or slides up to the major 3rd. The flat 5th leans up to the 5th.

The chord decides the job

The same note changes job when the chord changes. F is tension over C7 and the root over F7. Land on a note that was tense a bar ago.

10 · Put it to work

Practice path

  1. Load the 12-bar blues. Pick C7 and find every release note in the window, then check yourself with the release-only box.
  2. Play each tension note in the table, hold it for two beats and resolve it to the target. Say the interval out loud.
  3. Run the drills for C7, then F7, then G7. Notice how the same fret shape changes job on each chord.
  4. Play one four-bar line from the plan. Start with long chord tones, climb through the build and land on the 3rd of the release bar.
  5. Pick a song you know, type in its chords and play along at a slow tempo. Build for three bars and land on the change.

11 · Quick answers

Tension and release

What is tension and release in a guitar solo?

Tension pulls. Release is the landing. Clash with the chord, then land on the root, 3rd or 5th.

Which notes create tension?

A half step above a chord tone, and notes outside the chord's scale. On a dominant chord: the 4th, the flat 3rd and the flat 5th.

Which notes give release?

The root, 3rd and 5th of the chord under you. The 3rd names the chord. The root ends the phrase. The 5th stays open.

How long should I hold the tension?

One to four beats, until the resolution is late. Land on beat 1 of the next bar, or on the chord change.

Can I build tension without outside notes?

Yes. Rhythm, register, volume and repetition do it with scale notes. Same phrase, faster and higher, then a chord tone.

How is this different from playing the 3rd?

The 3rd is the landing. The notes before it are the pull.
