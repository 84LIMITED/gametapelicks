01 · The shape

Major and minor 3rds on the neck

A major 3rd is 4 half steps above the root. A minor 3rd is 3. On guitar a half step is one fret, so the two 3rds sit side by side. C with E is C major. C with Eb is C minor. One fret changes the whole sound of the chord.

RootMajor 3rd, filledMinor 3rd, ring

Two shortcuts to find any 3rd

Go up 4 frets

From a root, the major 3rd sits 4 frets up the same string.

Go up a string, back a fret

From a root, the major 3rd also sits one string higher and one fret back. Crossing from the G string to the B string keeps the same fret.

Step down for minor

The minor 3rd always sits one fret below the major 3rd.

The 11-fret window is your hunting ground. Every 3rd shows up four to six times inside it, so there is always one near your hand.

06 · The approach

How Trey hunts 3rds

Phish fans hear the Mixolydian sound first. Listen to what he does on the chord change and the blues shows up. He lands on the 3rd, circles it with half steps, bends into it and lets the scale carry the phrase until the next change. Here are five habits that describe how he goes after the changes. They come from his chord-tone playing with Phish and on his own, and none of it is a note-for-note transcription.

The Mixolydian scale gives you the sound. The 3rd tells you where to go. Each habit below puts that in your hands.

1

Land on the 3rd

Play the 3rd on beat 1 of the new chord. The listener hears the change in your solo before the band finishes the bar.

2

Repeat a motif and shift it

Keep a short phrase the same shape and let the notes follow the chords. The same idea over a new chord sounds like one thought moving through the song.

3

Approach from a half step

Play the note a fret above and a fret below the 3rd just before beat 1, then land on it. Players call this an enclosure.

4

Bend into the 3rd

Bend the 2nd up a whole step to reach a major 3rd, or a half step to reach a minor 3rd. Bend a minor 3rd up a half step for the bluesy lift that major-leaning jams use.

5

Climb and hold

Walk up to the 3rd at the top of the phrase, hold it with vibrato and leave a little space before the next change.

08 · The pattern recognition

Four rules that carry to any progression

Learn to spot these while the band plays. Each one tells you where the next 3rd will be before the chord arrives.

Same root, major to minor

The 3rd drops one fret. C to Cm moves E to Eb. Cm to C moves Eb to E.

Same chord quality

The 3rd moves by the same interval as the root. G7 to F7 drops the root two frets and the 3rd from B to A. Em to Dm moves G to F. Slide the same shape.

Chords with a b7

When two roots sit a 4th or 5th apart, the b7 of the old chord sits a half step from the new 3rd. C7 to F7 moves Bb to A. G7 to C7 moves F to E. Dm7 to G7 moves C to B. The pattern runs through every ii V I.

Chords without a 3rd

Sus chords and power chords leave the 3rd out. Hold the last 3rd you played, or wait for the chord to resolve and land on the 3rd there.

09 · Put it to work

Practice path

  1. Pick a root and a window. Find every major and minor 3rd without looking, then check yourself against the map.
  2. Load the 12-bar blues. Play the 3rds as whole notes on each change, following the route.
  3. Add the approach tone from the inspector one beat before each change.
  4. Add the descending cell, then the enclosure, from the drill section.
  5. Type in a song you know. Play along with the record at a slow tempo and land the 3rd on every change. Move the window up or down and run it again.

10 · Quick answers

Questions about 3rds on guitar

What is the 3rd of a chord?

The 3rd is the note 3 or 4 half steps above the root. Four half steps makes a major 3rd and three makes a minor 3rd. It decides whether the chord sounds major or minor.

How do I find the 3rd on the guitar?

Go up 4 frets on the same string from the root for the major 3rd, or go up one string and back one fret. Drop one fret from the major 3rd for the minor 3rd. From the G string to the B string, the major 3rd sits on the same fret.

Why aim for the 3rd in a solo?

The 3rd defines the chord. When you land on it as the chord changes, the listener hears your line follow the harmony.

What is an enclosure?

An enclosure is two notes played before your target, one a fret above and one a fret below. You resolve to the target note on the beat.

Does this work for minor keys and other progressions?

Yes. The lesson reads minor, 7th, maj7, m7, m7b5, dim, aug and sus chords, and the four pattern rules apply to any progression.
