01 · The shape

Major and minor 3rds on the neck

A major 3rd sits 4 half steps above the root and a minor 3rd sits 3. On the guitar one half step is one fret, so the two 3rds are neighbors. C with E is C major. C with Eb is C minor. One fret changes the mood of the whole chord.

RootMajor 3rd, filledMinor 3rd, ring

Two shortcuts to find any 3rd

Go up 4 frets

From a root, the major 3rd sits 4 frets up the same string.

Go up a string, back a fret

From a root, the major 3rd also sits one string higher and one fret back. Crossing from the G string to the B string keeps the same fret.

Step down for minor

The minor 3rd always sits one fret below the major 3rd.

The 11-fret window is your hunting ground. Every 3rd appears four to six times inside it, so you can always pick the one nearest your hand.

06 · The approach

How Trey hunts 3rds

Phish fans hear the Mixolydian color first. Listen for what the notes do on the chord change and a blues player appears: Trey lands on the 3rd, frames it with half steps and bends into it, then lets the scale carry the phrase until the next change. Five habits describe how he attacks the changes. They come from his chord-tone playing across Phish and his solo work, and the lesson contains no note-for-note transcription.

The Mixolydian scale supplies the color and the 3rd supplies the direction. Each habit below puts that direction in your hands.

1

Land on the 3rd

The 3rd arrives on beat 1 of the change. The listener hears the new chord in your solo before the band finishes the bar line.

2

Repeat a motif and shift it

A short cell keeps its shape while the notes follow the harmony. The same degrees over a new chord sound like one idea moving through the song.

3

Approach from a half step

A note one fret above and one fret below the 3rd, played just before beat 1, wraps around the target. Players call this an enclosure.

4

Bend into the 3rd

Bend the 2nd up a whole step into a major 3rd, or a half step into a minor 3rd. Bend a minor 3rd up a half step for the bright, bluesy lift that major-leaning jams use.

5

Climb and hold

Walk up to the 3rd at the top of the phrase, hold it with vibrato and leave space before the next change.

08 · The pattern recognition

Four rules that carry to any progression

These are the moves to recognize while the band is playing. Each one lets you place the next 3rd before the chord arrives.

Same root, major to minor

The 3rd drops one fret. C to Cm moves E to Eb. Cm to C moves Eb to E.

Same chord quality

The 3rd moves by the same interval as the root. G7 to F7 drops the root two frets and the 3rd from B to A. Em to Dm moves G to F. Slide the same shape.

Chords with a b7

When two roots sit a 4th or 5th apart, the b7 of the old chord sits a half step from the new 3rd. C7 to F7 moves Bb to A. G7 to C7 moves F to E. Dm7 to G7 moves C to B. The pattern runs through every ii V I.

Chords without a 3rd

Sus chords and power chords leave the 3rd out. Hold the last 3rd you played, or wait for the chord to resolve and land on the 3rd there.

09 · Put it to work

Practice path

  1. Pick a root and a window. Find every major and minor 3rd without looking, then check with the map.
  2. Load the 12-bar blues. Play the 3rds as whole notes on each change, following the route.
  3. Add the approach tone from the inspector one beat before each change.
  4. Add the descending cell, then the enclosure, from the drill section.
  5. Type a song you know. Play along with the record at a slow tempo and land the 3rd on every change. Move the window up or down and run it again.
